# Descriptions of textures



The Stylized textures generally follow a "hand-painted" or "cartoonish" aesthetic, characterized by exaggerated details, soft gradients, and clean, chunky silhouettes.



## Stylized Wood Planks 003

A clean, "World of Warcraft" style flooring. It features wide, horizontal wooden boards with exaggerated grain lines and soft beveled edges.

The colors are warm browns with highlights on the edges to give them a "chunky," hand-carved look.



## Stylized Wooden Roof 001

Composed of overlapping wooden shingles that vary slightly in size and rotation. The texture has a weathered but whimsical feel,

with visible wood grain and small nicks at the bottom of each shingle, typical of a medieval fantasy cottage.



## Stylized Metal Plates 001

Heavy, dark iron plates bolted together with oversized rivets. The metal has a soft,

painted sheen rather than a realistic metallic reflection, featuring scuffs and scratches that look like brushstrokes.



## Stylized Wall 002

A plaster or stucco wall texture with a slightly uneven, "wobbly" surface.

It includes subtle cracks and color variations (warm off-white or beige) that suggest a hand-painted architectural finish common in stylized RPG environments.



## Stylized Wood Wall 001

Vertical wooden planks used for siding. Unlike the floor planks,

these are more rustic with larger gaps and prominent "knots" in the wood, painted with high-contrast shadows to make the depth pop.



## Stylized Roof 001

A thick, bundled straw or thatch texture. It is arranged in heavy, rounded layers that give the impression of a cozy, rustic roof.

The "strands" are thick and simplified, resembling painted golden-yellow bundles.



## Stylized Cliff Rock 006

Sharp, angular rock faces with flat planes and jagged edges. The texture uses "edge highlighting" to define the silhouette of the rocks,

making them look like they were sculpted for a modern 3D platformer.



## Stylized Stone Floor 010

Large, irregular paving stones set in dark earth or grout.

The stones have smooth, rounded tops and "painterly" highlights on the edges, creating a clean, walkable path for a fantasy village.



## Stylized Stone Floor 009

Similar to 010 but with a more "cracked" and weathered appearance.

The stones are flatter, and the gaps between them are filled with subtle moss or dirt, giving it an older, more ancient ruin aesthetic.



## Stylized Rocks 003

Individual, chunky boulders and stones embedded in a ground surface.

The rocks have a very "smooth-yet-sharp" look, with clear directional lighting painted into the texture to define their 3D volume.



## Stylized Metal Pattern 001

A decorative metal grate or panel with an embossed geometric pattern.

It looks like forged fantasy armor or a dungeon door, featuring deep shadows in the recesses and bright "metal" highlights on the raised patterns.



## Stylized Wood Floor 001

A polished, interior wooden parquet or plank floor.

The finish is smoother than the outdoor versions, with a soft specular glow that suggests a well-maintained tavern or castle room.



## Stylized Metal Shingles 001

Overlapping metallic tiles, perhaps for a high-end fantasy tower or a steampunk structure.

The shingles have a bluish-grey steel tint with bright "edge-wear" painted onto the bottom lips.



## Stylized Feathers 002

A soft, organic texture consisting of overlapping bird feathers.

The individual barbs of the feathers are simplified into clean lines, creating a plumage effect that looks soft and fluffy rather than hyper-detailed.



## Stylized Stone Floor 007

Large, rectangular slabs of stone.

This texture is more formal and "built," ideal for a castle courtyard or a temple floor.

It features very clean lines and minimal weathering.



## Stylized Stone Floor 001b

A variation of the basic stone floor with a lighter, creamier color palette.

The stones are tightly packed with very thin grout lines, giving a bright and airy feel to the environment.



## Stylized Cliff Rock 005

A more vertical rock texture designed for canyon walls.

It features heavy horizontal "sedimentary" layers that have been simplified into thick, chunky bands with soft color gradients.



## Stylized Bricks 005

Classic red-toned fantasy bricks.

The bricks are slightly "wonky"—not perfectly straight—with thick, light-colored mortar.

Each brick has a unique hand-painted highlight to make it look individual.



## Stylized Terracotta Tiles 001

Bright orange-red clay tiles arranged in a grid or diamond pattern.

They have a warm, Mediterranean feel with soft rounded corners and subtle "baking" stains painted into the clay.







## Stylized Bark 003

A chunky, vertical tree bark texture. It features deep, stylized grooves and wide ridges that look like they were carved with a thick brush. The color palette includes varying shades of brown and ochre, with bright edge-highlights that give the bark a rugged, "gnarled" appearance suitable for an enchanted forest. 



## Stylized Ground 002

A soft, earthy floor texture that looks like packed dirt or forest floor. It has a smooth, almost velvety finish with subtle patches of lighter and darker brown. The highlights are very soft, giving it a clean, "baked" look without the noise of realistic gravel or pebbles. 



## Stylized Wood Shingles 001

Overlapping wooden roof tiles with a very distinct hand-painted feel. Each shingle has vertical grain lines and slight imperfections, like chips or rounded corners, painted in. The shadows beneath each tile are exaggerated to make the roof layers "pop" from a distance. 



## Stylized Wood Planks 001

Classic hand-painted floor or wall planks. These include painted-on nails or rivets at the ends of the boards. The wood grain is simplified into long, sweeping strokes, and the boards vary in tone to create a vibrant, non-uniform surface that feels lived-in and cozy. 



## Stylized Cliff Rock 001

A rock face texture defined by flat, "faceted" planes. It looks like a painting of a cliffside where the artist used a palette knife—sharp edges, clean color transitions, and deep crevices. It lacks fine-grain noise, making it look very clean and readable for game environments. 



## Wood Planks 006 Handpainted

A more rustic version of the wood plank style. This texture features heavier weathering and more contrasting grain patterns. The edges of the planks are slightly uneven, suggesting aged, reclaimed wood used in a medieval or fantasy setting. The color saturation is a bit higher, emphasizing the "cartoon" nature of the material.



## Cobblestone 001

> **Non-stylized.** This is a photorealistic PBR texture. Use only where a realistic stone ground is intentional; it will look out of place next to hand-painted stylized surfaces.

Tightly packed, rounded-square cobblestones in a photorealistic PBR style. The stones vary from near-white to mid-gray with subtle hints of lilac, separated by dark grout seams. Suitable for stone paths, plazas, and castle courtyards. Full PBR set: COLOR, NORMAL, ROUGHNESS, AO, DISPLACEMENT.



## Metal Armor Pattern 001

An overlapping fish-scale or dragon-scale pattern in medium gray steel, with rust-brown staining visible at the gaps between scales. The raised scale edges catch light cleanly. Use for armored surfaces, fantasy creature skin overlays, scale-mail armor props, or decorative metal cladding. Full PBR set.



## Metal Pattern 010

> **Non-stylized / borderline.** The geometric pattern is clean and abstract enough to work as a decorative panel or grate in stylized contexts, but the rendering is realistic rather than hand-painted. Use with intention.

An isometric cube / rhombus lattice in dark gray steel. The geometric pattern has an Art Deco or modernist architectural feel — repeating "3D cube illusion" diamond shapes outlined by fine dark lines. Includes an opacity map that exposes the lattice frame as a cutout, making it suitable for transparent grate or panel effects. Full PBR set with opacity.



## Stylized Bricks 004

Irregular, roughly-cut stone bricks in warm gray-brown, heavily colonized by patches of bright green moss growing in and around the mortar joints. More organic and weathered than Bricks 005 — ideal for ruins, dungeon walls, or outdoor fantasy structures where visible overgrowth adds age. Stylized PBR set.



## Stylized Roof Tiles 001

Rounded barrel (mission-style) clay roof tiles in warm rust and dark brown, with painted-on weathering — yellow-green staining and variable color per tile. The rounded profile is clearly stylized. Pairs well with Mediterranean, Spanish colonial, or warm-climate fantasy architecture. Stylized PBR set.



## Stylized Thatched Roof 003

Dense golden-yellow thatch composed of tight horizontal bundles of straw, with individual strand detail visible. More naturalistic than the other thatched roof textures — less cartoon-exaggerated, suitable where a grounded look is needed within a stylized world. Stylized PBR set.



## White Marble 006

> **Non-stylized.** Full PBR fidelity, not hand-painted. Works best in scenes where a clean, neutral stone is needed (palace floors, columns, fountain bases) and no stylized marble alternative exists.

Clean, light white marble with thin diagonal gray-blue veining. The veins are fine and sparse, giving a high-end minimal look. Not stylized — full PBR fidelity.

## Noise Textures (`noise/`)

Seamlessly tiling greyscale utility textures for use in custom WGSL shaders. Assign to a `texture_0`–`texture_3` slot in your material's RON definition and sample in the shader. All files tile seamlessly.

### When to use each type

| Type | Visual character | Best use cases |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Blue noise | Random-looking dots with visually uniform distribution | Dithering, temporal AA offsets, stipple shading, random sampling |
| Gabor noise | Directional stripes at a fixed angle and frequency | Fiber textures, woven fabric, wood grain, anisotropic surface detail |
| Perlin noise | Smooth, organic cloud-like blobs | Clouds, smoke, terrain blending, dissolve masks, general organic noise |
| Value noise | Similar to Perlin but with harder, blockier transitions | Roughness variation, crumbling surfaces, less smooth than Perlin |
| Voronoi noise | Cell/mosaic pattern — regions separated by sharp edges | Cracked earth, dragon scales, mosaic effects, cell shading |

### File reference

**Blue noise**
- `seamless-blue-noise-64.png` — 64×64. Smallest and fastest; use when UV precision is low or performance is critical.
- `seamless-blue-noise-128.png` — 128×128. Better quality; preferred default for dithering effects.

**Gabor noise** — directional band-limited noise. Both files share the same parameters (3000 kernels, frequency 0.08, 60° orientation, sigma 12); choose by resolution.
- `seamless-gabor-512_...png` — 512×512. Use on smaller or more distant surfaces.
- `seamless-gabor-1024_...png` — 1024×1024. Preferred for close-up or large surfaces.

**Perlin noise** — smooth gradient noise. "Cells" controls feature size: more cells = finer detail.
- `seamless-perlin-noise-512x512-8-cells.png` — 512×512, 8 cells. Coarsest variant; large blobby shapes.
- `seamless-perlin-noise-1024x1024-8-cells.png` — 1024×1024, 8 cells. Same feature size, higher resolution.
- `seamless-perlin-noise-1024x1024-12-cells.png` — 1024×1024, 12 cells. Finer detail; preferred for dissolve masks and surface blending.

**Value noise** — blockier than Perlin.
- `seamless-value-noise-512x512-16-cells.png` — 512×512, 16 cells. Good for roughness variation and crumbling-edge effects.

**Voronoi noise** — cell pattern. More cells = smaller, denser cells.
- `seamless-voronoi-noise-512x512-12-cells.png` — 512×512, 12 cells. Coarse cells; use for large cracked-earth or reptile-scale surfaces.
- `seamless-voronoi-noise-1024x1024-12-cells.png` — 1024×1024, 12 cells. Same feature size, higher resolution.
- `seamless-voronoi-noise-1024x1024-24-cells.png` — 1024×1024, 24 cells. Finest variant; suitable for detailed scales or small-cell mosaic effects.
